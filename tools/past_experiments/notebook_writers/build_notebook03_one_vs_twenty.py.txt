## Interpretation

The encoder maps each complete graph frame—node positions, connectivity, edge geometry and spring stiffnesses—to two numbers, z0 and z1. It is not given the optimization label or the p-ratio.

With one training network per source, the easiest solution is to encode the broad network type and the dominant compression motion. The two latent coordinates are therefore largely redundant and the trajectory is nearly a straight line.

With twenty networks per source, network type alone is insufficient. The encoder must also represent differences among networks of the same type and changes in their transverse response during compression, so the two coordinates carry less duplicated information and the trajectory can curve.

Node-optimized networks differ mainly through node geometry and edge lengths; stiffness-optimized networks differ mainly through their spring-stiffness pattern. Because those quantities enter the graph encoder, the two families are mapped to different latent ranges. The axes themselves are learned coordinates—not fixed physical labels such as “geometry” and “stiffness.”
"""),

The encoder receives each low-T graph frame's node displacements together with its static reference positions, edge geometry, connectivity and spring stiffnesses. It never receives optimization label or p-ratio.
